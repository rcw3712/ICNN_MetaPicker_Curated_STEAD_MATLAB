Journal: Earth Science Informatics

Title: Waveform Identity and Reproducible Source-Grouped Evaluation of Seismic Phase Pickers

Abstract
Reliable comparison of seismic phase pickers requires traceable data identities and inspectable relationships between training observations and derived predictions. We present a reproducible evaluation workflow linking waveform fingerprints, earthquake-source groups, out-of-fold feature construction, model provenance and numerical verification. An exhaustive audit of the local STanford EArthquake Dataset archive uniquely matches 2,234 retained waveforms to 1,477 sources. A tested export profile also regenerates all 2,234 retained CSV byte hashes. A fixed source-disjoint split contains 330 test records from 221 sources. Fresh replay loads all 106 checkpoints, reproduces 18,528 out-of-fold and 1,980 test feature tensors, and recovers 70 test prediction sets; independent calculations reproduce 1,260 metric rows. Synthetic examples demonstrate rejection of invalid source membership, augmentation lineage, feature order and artifact bytes. Temporal stacking and a PhaseNet-style baseline provide a case study of component access. On this fixed split, stacking achieves accepted-pick F1 scores within 100 ms of 0.8268/0.5536 for compressional/shear arrivals with three components and 0.8134/0.2686 with vertical input alone. The shear-phase difference is 0.2850, with a conditional source-cluster 95% interval of 0.2293–0.3418. The baseline has a supported compressional-phase advantage; primary shear-phase method-comparison intervals include zero. The contribution is an executable connection between identity, data lineage and evaluation, rather than a new stacking algorithm. Historical development exposure prevents an independent-confirmation claim. One fixed split, limited unseen-station observations and unresolved historical selection decisions constrain predictive interpretation.

Keywords: seismic phase picking; waveform identity; data provenance; source-grouped evaluation; reproducibility; temporal stacking
